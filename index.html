01 Describe a supplier negotiation you handled and the result.

My direct negotiation experience has mainly involved service suppliers, transport providers, and operating partners rather than raw-material manufacturers.

During the 3PL transition serving Central Retail Vietnam at an approximately 60,000 m² ambient and cold-chain distribution center, delivery windows, receiving capacity, temperature requirements, escalation responsibilities, and SLA expectations had to be aligned across service providers, suppliers, warehouse teams, and the customer.

I prepared the operational data, identified non-negotiable requirements and available trade-offs, and worked with the parties to agree schedules, ownership, escalation rules, and corrective actions. The result was a controlled ramp-up with service continuity while the operation handled up to 18,000 pallets and approximately 800 tons per day.

02 How would you track vendors, quotes, samples, and inventory?

I would begin with a process map, data dictionary, ownership rules, and unique IDs linking every vendor, material, quotation, sample, and purchase order.

  • Vendor master: category, contacts, manufacturer/distributor status, certificates, MOQ, terms, lead time, Incoterms, risk, and qualification.
  • Material master: specification, grade, application, pack size, certificates, alternatives, and storage requirements.
  • RFQ and quotes: price, currency, MOQ, freight, terms, lead time, validity, sample status, and estimated landed cost.
  • Samples: requested, confirmed, shipped, received, tested, approved/rejected, tracking, and follow-up.
  • Inventory: PO status, expected delivery, receipts, discrepancies, stock, incoming quantity, usage, and reorder point.

The first version can use a maintainable spreadsheet, database, or low-code platform selected with the client, supported by a dashboard, alerts, SOP, and named data owners.

05 How long would sourcing three suppliers and requesting samples take?

Sodium Coco-Sulfate6–8 active hours · normally 2–4 business days

Lactobacillus14–20 active hours · normally 5–10 business days

Lactobacillus requires confirmation of strain, application, grade, CFU count, storage, shelf life, certificates, and regulatory requirements before suppliers can be compared.

  1. Confirm the specification, quantity, destination, certificates, target price, and Incoterms.
  2. Build a longlist: normally 6–8 candidates for a common material and 10–15 for a specification-sensitive material.
  3. Verify manufacturer/distributor status, registration, certificates, TDS, SDS, COA, traceability, and capacity.
  4. Send a consistent written RFQ and sample request to qualified candidates.
  5. Compare price, MOQ, freight, payment, lead time, validity, documents, and estimated landed cost.
  6. Follow up until three valid written quotes are received, request samples, and record every action.

These estimates cover active sourcing work. Supplier response time, sample production, shipping, customs, and laboratory testing are excluded.
